## About Me

I am a PhD Researcher in Economics at the **Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee, India**. My research lies at the intersection of **health economics, labour economics, and applied microeconomics**, with a particular focus on understanding healthcare expenditure, financial protection, and the socioeconomic determinants of health.

My current research focuses on **healthcare financing and out-of-pocket expenditure**, including the economic burden associated with diseases and occupational health conditions. I am particularly interested in how healthcare costs affect households and workers, and in identifying socioeconomic and institutional factors that shape access to healthcare and financial protection.

## Current Research

My ongoing research examines the economic dimensions of healthcare and occupational health in India. I work with large-scale household and health survey datasets as well as primary survey data to investigate healthcare expenditure, healthcare utilisation, financial hardship, and socioeconomic disparities.

I am particularly interested in applying **causal inference and econometric methods** to address selection bias, confounding, and heterogeneity in observational data.

## Research Approach

My research combines economic theory with empirical methods to address policy-relevant questions. I use quantitative techniques including regression analysis, propensity score methods, willingness-to-pay estimation, and other applied econometric approaches.

I am interested in producing evidence that can contribute to better health financing policies, improved financial protection, and more equitable access to healthcare.
